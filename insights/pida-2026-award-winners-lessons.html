Awards

PIDA 2026: what the winning packs have in common

The Australasian Packaging Innovation & Design Awards were handed out in Melbourne in July. Read together, the winners are a fair preview of what a good brief looks like next year.

The 2026 Australasian Packaging Innovation & Design (PIDA) Awards were presented in Melbourne on 28 July, alongside the foodpro exhibition. Run by the Australasian Institute of Packaging, PIDA is the only route for Australian and New Zealand packs into the WorldStar Packaging Awards, which makes it the closest thing the region has to an official verdict on what good packaging looks like right now.

PIDA is not a graphic design competition in the way Pentawards or the AGDA Awards are. Entries are assessed on how a pack performs: structure, materials, recoverability and usability, as much as appearance. That is exactly why the results are useful to brand owners. They show where the industry's own experts have set the bar, and that bar shapes what retailers, regulators and shoppers come to expect of a pack.

The headline winners

In food, gold went to Primo Foods and Amcor Flexibles for a peel-and-reseal sliced meats pack built on a recycle-ready mono-material PET structure with a 10 per cent reduction in plastic. In beverages, Graphic Packaging International and Asahi Beverages won for a kerbside recyclable fibre wrap that replaces plastic shrink film on Carlton Dry six-packs.

The Health, Beauty & Wellness category, which covers cosmetics, personal care, supplements and medical packaging, was won by Impact International for an Australian-made mono-material HDPE tube and cap range. The design replaces the usual polyethylene tube and polypropylene cap combination with a single polymer so the whole pack can enter the HDPE recycling stream, and it carries between 30 and 50 per cent Australian-made post-consumer recycled content depending on size. Silver went to ProAmpac New Zealand for outer packaging with 50 per cent recycled LDPE, and bronze to the Esmi Cleanser Butter Balm.

Simplot's Birds Eye frozen vegetables took gold in Save Food Packaging for an 825 gram block-bottom bag with a resealable tape strip and 50 per cent recycled-content polyethylene. Yates won Domestic & Household for a contact-free refill system that removes direct handling of garden chemicals.

Two entries collected multiple medals. Bonson Packaging's RePlay Returnables, a borrow-and-return container system, won three golds across Labelling & Decoration, Marketing Design and Reuse. EcoMark Natural Branding, a collaboration between Costa Group, Result Group and The Packaging Hippie, won gold for Resource Reduction and silver in both Labelling & Decoration and Marketing Design.

Sustainability stopped being a category

The organisers' own read of the results was that sustainability is no longer a category in its own right. It has become the expectation that sits under every category. The Sustainable Packaging Design award was split five ways this year, with golds for resource reduction, reuse, recycled content, design for recovery and innovation in resource recovery, on the basis that there is no single route to a better pack. The practical lesson for a brand is to pick the principle that suits the product and be able to evidence it, rather than claiming all five on the front panel.

The most useful changes were small

Neither of the food golds is a radical pack. The Birds Eye bag is a familiar format with a reseal strip and a flat bottom, designed to stop frozen vegetables spilling in the freezer and to make it easy to use part of a bag and put the rest back. The Primo pack peels open and closes again. Both improve the pack without asking the shopper to change how they buy, cook or store. The change that wins is often structural before it is graphic, and a designer who can see that is worth more than one who only refreshes the front panel.

Decoration is being asked to do more

The Labelling & Decoration gold did not go to a beautiful label. It went to a dishwasher-resistant in-mould label that carries a unique scannable code on every RePlay container, so each one can be tracked through purchase, return, cleaning and reuse for up to 50 cycles. The EcoMark silver went the other way: the avocado skin itself became the media. During April 2025, Costa's Lovacado avocados were laser-etched with Easter characters in 20 Woolworths stores across Victoria, with the standard PLU sticker left in place and nothing added to the fruit. The campaign avoided another sleeve, tag or label entirely.

For branding agencies the signal is clear. The label is expected to carry identity, traceability and sometimes the entire promotion, and the strongest entries treated it as part of the pack's function rather than a surface applied at the end.

Accessibility is now a scoring criterion

ByStorm Beauty won the Accessible & Inclusive Packaging award for adaptive makeup tools and a mailer box developed with people living with disability, with intuitive opening features, braille and information presented in more than one format. The Honey Squeeze Pack from Caps & Closures took silver in the same category and gold for Design for Recovery, proof that openability and recyclability can live in one design. Ease of opening, legible type and one-handed use are becoming judged qualities, not courtesies.

Beauty and wellness had a strong podium

Recyclable tubes have been an unsolved problem for cosmetic and supplement brands for years, largely because the tube and the cap were made from different polymers. The Impact International win matters because it is a local, commercially available answer to one of the most common recyclability failures in the category, with the recycled content in a layer that never touches the product. Any cosmetic or supplement brand planning a range refresh should now put the tube-and-cap question to the designer and the filler at the same time.

The pathway is real

Every PIDA category winner is eligible for the 2027 WorldStar Packaging Awards. In the 2026 WorldStar round, announced in January, seven Australian and New Zealand companies collected 11 awards from a field of 481 entries across 36 countries, among them the Koor refillable system and the Carman's Gourmet Snack Mix from Carman's Kitchen and Zipform Packaging. Regional recognition travels.

Timing is the catch. Entries for the 2026 program were open in January and closed on 27 February, so a pack that hopes to be in the 2027 running needs to be launched, photographed and documented by then.
