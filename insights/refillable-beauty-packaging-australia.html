Refillable beauty packaging: what Australian brands are doing

Refills have moved from niche to normal across Australian and New Zealand beauty. The design challenge is no longer whether to offer one, but how to make the system feel premium and the claims hold up.

Short answer: Refillable beauty packaging is now a mainstream format in Australia, built on three repeatable systems: pods and inserts, magnetic pans and refill pouches. Each changes what the outer pack has to do, and each carries a claims risk that the ACCC has already spelled out.

A February 2026 round-up by Fashion Journal, "Fill it up: The Australian and New Zealand beauty brands offering refills", lists more than a dozen local labels with some form of refill option. The list is useful less as a ranking than as a catalogue of the design systems in use. Looking across it, the brands cluster into a small number of approaches, and each one asks something different of the designer.

Three refill systems, three design problems

Pods inside a durable outer

Kora Organics offers three refillable products, including its Active Algae Moisturiser, each arriving in a glass jar with a removable, recyclable pod, according to the Fashion Journal round-up. The outer jar becomes the permanent brand asset, so it carries more of the premium cues: weight, finish, closure and a graphic that still looks considered on a bathroom shelf in year three. The pod, by contrast, is a consumable, and its job is to be easy to swap without touching product.

Magnetic pans for colour cosmetics

Makeup has led the way with pans. Fluff Cosmetics sells its Lip Oil and Bronzing Powder in magnetic refill pans, and the article notes that the used pans are recyclable. Ere Perez offers refillable pans for bronzers, blushes and eye colours, and Eye of Horus sells some compacts empty so customers can build their own palette. For designers, pans shift the brand moment to the compact and the refill carton. The compact must feel worth keeping, while the refill packaging has to make the right shade unmistakable at a glance.

Refill pouches for bottles and jars

Foile pairs a glass bottle with a refill pouch for each product, and Hunter Lab positions its glass "forever bottles" as vessels to be topped up. Grown Alchemist offers Eco Refills for best-selling products, and Leif offers refills in different sizes across its range. Pouches are the most economical format, but they put a weaker, more utilitarian pack next to a premium one, so the pouch graphics need to read as part of the same system rather than as a lesser sibling.

What the refill changes in the brief

A refill is not a smaller version of the original. It is a second pack that must be recognisable, correctly matched to the right outer, and clear about how to use it. A sound brief for a refillable range should settle a few things early:

  • Compatibility. Which refill fits which outer, and how a customer can tell. Colour coding, shape keys and clear naming prevent the wrong pod ending up in the wrong jar.
  • Instructions. Short, visual steps on the refill itself. Most customers will do this once, at home, without help.
  • Range architecture. New shades, formulas and seasonal drops should slot into the same outer without a redesign.
  • The empty state. Some brands sell outers or compacts empty. The unfilled pack still has to look finished.

Keep the claims honest

Refill messaging sits in the part of the pack where regulators pay attention. The ACCC's guidance, "A guide to making environmental claims for business", released on 12 December 2023, sets out eight principles. In the ACCC's wording they include: make accurate and truthful claims; have evidence to back up your claims; don't hide important information; explain any conditions on claims; avoid broad and unqualified claims; use clear and easy to understand language; visual elements shouldn't give the wrong impression; and be direct and open about sustainability transition.

Applied to refills, that suggests a few practical habits. Avoid unqualified phrases such as "eco-friendly" or "zero waste" on a refillable range, and say specifically what the refill does, for example that a pod replaces the jar insert. Check that green colour palettes, leaf icons and similar visual cues are not implying more than the evidence supports. If a component such as a pump or pod is recyclable only in some circumstances, say so, and use the Australasian Recycling Label correctly rather than improvising a claim.

What to take from this

The local examples show that refills work best when the system is designed as a whole: a durable outer worth keeping, a refill that is obvious to match and easy to fit, and claims that stay specific. Brands that treat the refill as an afterthought tend to end up with two packs that do not look like they belong together.
